Every object you upload is copied before S3 says 200.

One PUT lands as copies in of the Region. Only once every copy is written does S3 answer 200 OK. Tap the chip to see what One Zone-IA keeps instead.

Two promises

11 nines durability, 99.99 % availability — not the same thing.

Now . The copies there go dark, the others still serve the read. Durability is about bytes surviving for years; availability is about answering right now, and S3 may say 503 while it heals.

Versioning

S3 never loses bytes. It cheerfully deletes what you tell it to.

Tap . With the delete only drops a 0-byte marker on top; the old version sits behind it, still billed, one call from undeleted. Turn versioning off and try again.

Storage classes

Same bytes, five prices.

Age the object with (tap or drag). Lifecycle rules walk it downstairs; every shelf below Standard has a minimum stay you pay for even if you delete early, and the cheaper shelves charge to read or make you wait.

Intelligent-Tiering

Don't know the access pattern? Let S3 move it.

After 30 idle days the object drops to Infrequent, after 90 to Archive Instant. Now : it climbs straight back to Frequent, with no retrieval fee and no minimum stay. You pay a small monitoring fee per object instead of guessing.

Replication

Copy to another Region for disaster recovery.

Turn on : every new version is copied, asynchronously, to a bucket in us-east-1. It needs versioning on both buckets, an IAM role, and if the business wants a number, RTC promises 99.99 % within 15 minutes.
